# Yakima Valley Weekend — Great Prosser Balloon Rally & Wine Country

> **Trip Dates:** September 25–27, 2026 (4th full weekend in September)
> **Drive from Seattle:** ~3 hours via I-90 E → WA-24 E / I-82 E
> **Event:** 37th Annual Great Prosser Balloon Rally (Sept 25–27) + Prosser Harvest Festival

---

## Overview

This trip centers on the **Great Prosser Balloon Rally** in Prosser, WA — the longest-running balloon rally in the Pacific Northwest. 20+ hot air balloons launch at sunrise each day from the Prosser Airport over the Horse Heaven Hills and Yakima River valley. The rally runs the same weekend as the **Prosser Harvest Festival** (downtown street vendors, street painting, local crafts). Prosser is also the birthplace of Washington's wine industry with 30+ wineries in a walkable downtown.

**Note:** The Great Prosser Balloon Rally is the premier balloon event in the Yakima Valley. There is no separate "Yakima balloon festival" or "Posse balloon festival" — the Prosser Rally is the event.

### Saturday Hike Options

#### Option A: Cowiche Canyon Trail (Recommended)
- **Distance:** 2.9 miles (canyon floor trail) or up to 6.3 mi out-and-back
- **Elevation:** Minimal on canyon floor
- **Difficulty:** Easy
- **Duration:** 1–2 hours
- **Features:** Old railroad bed, Cowiche Creek, basalt cliffs, 9 creek crossings
- **Best in September:** Fall colors, comfortable temps
- **Trailhead:** Multiple access points. Cowiche Canyon East Trailhead on Cowiche Road or downtown trailhead near Cowiche Canyon Kitchen

#### Option B: Cowiche Canyon Uplands Trail
- **Distance:** 3–4 miles (various loop options)
- **Elevation:** ~400 ft gain
- **Difficulty:** Moderate
- **Duration:** 1.5–2.5 hours
- **Features:** Shrub-steppe habitat, views toward Mount Adams (clear days), wild sagebrush
- **Good for:** Shorter, more exposed hike with panoramic views

#### Option C: Ahtanum Ridge Trail
- **Distance:** 3–5 miles (loop options)
- **Elevation:** Moderate gain
- **Difficulty:** Moderate
- **Duration:** 2–3 hours
- **Features:** Ridge-top views, rolling hills, less crowded
- **Best for:** More serious hikers who want solitude

## Plan B — Weather Contingency

Balloon launches are **weather-dependent**. If skies are overcast or winds are too high:

- **Saturday morning alternative:** Skip Prosser, do the Ahtanum Ridge hike first (morning light is best), then wine tasting in Prosser in the afternoon (balloon prep might still happen later in the day if weather clears)
- **If all balloon launches cancel:** Focus on wine trail (Prosser has the best concentration of wineries in WA), Prosser Harvest Festival, and Cowiche Canyon trails. Consider a detour to **Grandview** (20 min north of Prosser) for the **Yakima Valley Fair** grounds and local orchard stands (September = apple season)
- **Indoor fallback:** Yakima has several craft breweries — **Moonshine Brewing** (water balloon fights on select dates), **Tieton Brewing Co.**

## Hidden Gems & Lesser-Known Spots

- **Rocky Top trails** (Cowiche Canyon uplands) — Short loops with best valley views, often overlooked
- **Tieton River Nature Trail** — Easy walk along the Tieton River, less touristy than Cowiche
- **Grandview** — 20 min north of Prosser. Small town charm, local orchard stands in September, less crowded wine tasting rooms
- **Los Hernandez Tamales** — Not a restaurant, more of a cultural institution. Best tamales in Yakima County
- **Yakima River Rim Trail** — For serious hikers: 8.3-mile loop with dramatic canyon views (4+ hours)
- **Evergreen Family Wines** (Prosser) — "Wine Down Wednesday" and Thursday events — casual, affordable tastings

## Honorary Mentions

- **Yakima Valley College** — Vineyard Designated Wines program offers educational tours
- **Selah Butte** — Challenging 2.8-mile out-and-back with 1,640 ft elevation gain. Views of Yakima River Canyon. Note: Trailhead is unmarked and on private property — use with permission
- **Cowiche Creek** — The 9 creek crossings on the canyon trail are a hiker's delight (and occasional hazard in wet years)
- **Washington State's first AVA** — Yakima Valley AVA (established 1983) — the oldest in Washington

## Practical Tips

### Booking Advice
- **Hotels:** Book immediately. Festival weekend fills up fast. Sunnyside and Tri-Cities are backup lodging areas if Yakima is full.
- **Balloon flights:** If you want to actually fly (commercial flights offered by Seattle Ballooning), book early — limited spots each morning.
- **Restaurants:** Cowiche Canyon Kitchen and Crafted — make reservations for Saturday dinner.
- **Wineries:** No reservations needed for most Prosser wineries, but arrive early (11 AM–2 PM is peak). Some close by 5 PM.

### Local Transit
- **No public transit** in Yakima Valley. You need a car.
- **Prosser downtown** is walkable for wine tasting (12 wineries within walking distance), but you'll need your car to return to your hotel.
- **Designated driver:** If wine tasting heavily, designate one driver or use rideshare (limited coverage in Prosser).

### Packing & Weather
- **September in Yakima Valley:** Daytime 70–80°F, nights 45–55°F. Layering essential.
- **Balloon viewing:** Dress warm for 6:30 AM sunrise — mornings are cool, especially in September. Bring a hat, light jacket, thermos.
- **Hiking:** Bring water (desert climate, low humidity), sun protection, sturdy shoes for unpaved trails.
- **Gluten-free dining:** Call ahead to confirm cross-contamination protocols. Cowiche Canyon Kitchen and Jenny Mae's are the most GF-friendly. Jenny Mae's is a dedicated GF bakery — safest option for celiac travelers.
- **Camera:** Essential for balloon launches. Golden hour lighting.

### Seasonal Notes
- **September = apple season.** Expect orchard stands, apple cider, and local apple products throughout the valley.
- **Harvest season overlap:** Some wineries host special harvest events (grape stomps, blending workshops). Check individual winery calendars.
- **Prosser Harvest Festival** runs concurrently with the balloon rally — free event, great for browsing local crafts.
